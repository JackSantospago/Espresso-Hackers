Rice growing guide: lowland and upland rice, water, planting and main problems.

Rice systems: lowland (paddy) rice grows in flooded fields surrounded by small earth walls (bunds). Upland rice is grown without flooding, rain-fed like maize, on slopes and hills. (Source: Wikipedia)

Rice temperature and timing: rice is damaged by temperatures below about 12 °C, so plant when average daily temperatures are reliably above that. From seed to harvest usually takes several months, up to about six months for long-season varieties. (Source: Wikipedia)

Rice water saving: alternate wetting and drying (AWD) saves water in paddy rice: let the water level drop to about 15 cm below the soil surface, then flood again to about 5 cm. Flood-tolerant varieties (Sub1) survive about two weeks under water; drought- and salt-tolerant varieties also exist. (Source: Wikipedia)

Rice planting: direct seeding needs about 60 to 80 kg of seed per hectare; transplanting seedlings needs about 40 kg but more labour. (Source: Wikipedia)

Rice problems: rice blast (a fungus) and bacterial leaf streak are the worst rice diseases; stem borers, planthoppers, leafhoppers and rats are major pests. Overusing broad-spectrum insecticides can cause planthopper outbreaks by killing their natural enemies. (Source: Wikipedia)

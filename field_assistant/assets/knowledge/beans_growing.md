Common bean growing guide: climate, soil, planting, feeding, harvest and storage.

Bean climate: common beans grow best at about 17 to 27 °C. Above 30 °C flowers drop, and above 35 °C few seeds form. In the tropics they do best at about 600 to 1,950 m altitude. Beans need about 300 to 400 mm of well-spread rain per crop and dry weather at harvest; both drought and waterlogging harm them, and they are sensitive to frost. (Source: Infonet-Biovision)

Bean soil: beans like well-drained, light to medium soils with a pH close to neutral and good organic matter. They do not tolerate salty soils. (Source: Infonet-Biovision)

Bean types: bush beans form small upright plants 20 to 60 cm tall; climbing beans grow 2 to 3 m long vines and need stakes, but usually yield more per area. (Source: Wikipedia; Infonet-Biovision)

Bean planting: plant bush beans at about 30 cm between rows and 15 cm between plants, one seed per hole, 3 to 6 cm deep. Climbing beans are planted at about half that density. (Source: Infonet-Biovision)

Bean feeding: beans make their own nitrogen with the help of rhizobia bacteria in root nodules, so nitrogen fertiliser is usually not needed. They benefit from phosphorus, potassium, and compost or well-rotted manure, especially on hard soils. Beans leave nitrogen behind for the next crop. (Source: Infonet-Biovision; Wikipedia)

Bean harvest: harvest dry beans when most pods (about 80%) have turned yellow and dry. Dry the beans well in the sun to about 12% moisture before storage. (Source: Infonet-Biovision)

Bean food safety: raw or undercooked dry beans, especially red kidney beans, contain a natural toxin; soak and boil dry beans thoroughly (at least 30 minutes of boiling) before eating. (Source: Wikipedia)

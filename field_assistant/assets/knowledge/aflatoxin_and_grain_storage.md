Aflatoxin and safe grain storage: drying, storing and protecting maize, beans and groundnuts.

Aflatoxin is a poison made by moulds (Aspergillus flavus and Aspergillus parasiticus) that grow on maize, groundnuts, sorghum, millet, cassava, rice and other crops, in the field and in storage. It damages the liver, weakens the immune system, and is linked to stunted growth in children. Cooking does not destroy it. (Source: Wikipedia)

Aflatoxin risk factors: drought stress in the field, insect damage to grain, late harvesting, and storing grain that is not dry enough in warm, humid conditions. The mould grows best at about 27 to 30 °C. (Source: Wikipedia)

Aflatoxin prevention: harvest on time; dry grain quickly and well, on a tarpaulin or raised platform, not on bare soil; sort out and throw away mouldy, discoloured, shrivelled or insect-damaged grain (do not feed it to children); and store grain clean and dry. (Source: Wikipedia)

Safe grain storage moisture: dry maize to about 13% moisture or below before storage, and beans to about 12%. A moisture meter gives the most reliable reading. (Source: Purdue PICS study; Infonet-Biovision)

Hermetic storage bags (PICS bags): these are airtight bags with three layers. When they are tied properly, the grain and insects use up the oxygen inside, so weevils and other storage insects die without any chemicals. In one study, maize in PICS bags lost only about 2 to 3% of its weight over 24 weeks, compared with about 40% in ordinary woven bags. Tie each layer separately and check for holes. (Source: Purdue PICS study; Wikipedia)

Storing dry beans: sun-dry beans well, then mix them with ash or diatomite, or store them in airtight containers or hermetic bags, to stop bruchid weevils. (Source: Infonet-Biovision)

Nutrient deficiency signs in crops: how to read leaf colour.

Nutrient deficiency rule of thumb: look at which leaves show the problem first. Nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium and magnesium can move inside the plant, so shortage shows first on the older, lower leaves. Calcium, sulfur, iron, zinc, boron and manganese cannot move, so shortage shows first on the young, upper leaves. (Source: UConn Home & Garden Education Center)

Nitrogen deficiency: older lower leaves turn pale green to yellow, starting from the tip and along the midrib, and plants are small and grow slowly. Manure, compost, legumes in rotation, or nitrogen fertiliser correct it. (Source: UConn Home & Garden Education Center)

Phosphorus deficiency: older leaves and leaf edges turn purple or reddish, and plants are stunted with weak roots. It is common on tropical soils such as Nitisols and Andosols that lock up (fix) phosphorus. (Source: UConn Home & Garden Education Center; Wikipedia)

Potassium deficiency: the edges of older leaves turn yellow and then brown and dry, as if scorched, while the centre of the leaf stays green. This edge pattern distinguishes it from nitrogen shortage. (Source: UConn Home & Garden Education Center)

Sulfur and calcium deficiency: sulfur shortage makes young leaves light green or yellow, sometimes cupped. Calcium shortage makes young leaves curl and grow poorly, with dead patches. (Source: UConn Home & Garden Education Center)

Nutrient deficiency or disease? Deficiency colours are usually even and follow a pattern on many plants (whole leaf, edges, or between veins). Disease usually makes separate spots with clear edges, rings or powder. If unsure, show a sample to your extension officer. (Source: UConn Home & Garden Education Center)

Maize northern leaf blight (turcicum leaf blight): what it is, how to recognise it, how it spreads and what the farmer can do.

Maize northern leaf blight, also called turcicum leaf blight, is caused by the fungus Exserohilum turcicum (also called Setosphaeria turcica). In Africa it is most damaging in cooler, humid highland areas. Severe infection during silking and grain filling can cause more than 30% yield loss. (Source: PMC review of maize leaf pathogens in Africa)

Maize northern leaf blight symptoms: long, cigar-shaped or oval grey-green to tan spots, from about 2.5 cm to 15 cm or more long. They start as small flecks or narrow tan streaks and usually appear first on the lower leaves. In humid weather the spots look dark and sooty because of the spores. Spots can join and kill large parts of the leaf. (Source: Wikipedia; PMC review of maize leaf pathogens in Africa)

Maize northern leaf blight conditions: infection is favoured by moderate temperatures of about 18 to 27 °C with leaves wet for more than six hours, for example from long dew periods. The fungus survives on infected maize residue and spreads by wind and rain. One infection cycle takes 10 to 14 days on susceptible plants. (Source: Wikipedia)

Maize northern leaf blight damage: if the disease is severe on the upper leaves two to three weeks after silking, grain yield can fall by 40 to 70%. Infection after the grain has filled does much less damage. (Source: Wikipedia)

Maize northern leaf blight management: plant resistant varieties (with Ht resistance genes). Rotate maize with other crops for one to two years. Bury or remove infected residue after harvest. Adjust the planting date to avoid the most humid period. Weed control improves airflow. Fungicides are rarely economic for smallholders in Africa; ask your extension officer first. (Source: Wikipedia; PMC review of maize leaf pathogens in Africa)

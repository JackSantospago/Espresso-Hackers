Coffee leaf rust is caused by the fungus Hemileia vastatrix. Look for yellow-orange powdery spots on the underside of leaves. Badly infected trees drop their leaves, which reduces the next season's yield. It spreads fastest in warm, wet weather.

Managing coffee leaf rust: plant resistant varieties when replanting, prune for airflow, manage shade, and keep trees well nourished. Fungicide use and timing should be agreed with the extension officer or cooperative.

Coffee berry borer is a small beetle that bores into the berry. Look for a tiny round hole near the tip of green or ripe berries. Damaged beans lose weight and quality, which lowers the price.

Managing coffee berry borer: pick all ripe and over-ripe berries at harvest, collect berries that fell to the ground, and do not leave old berries on the trees after harvest. Traps can help monitor numbers.

Older coffee trees produce less. Rejuvenation pruning (stumping) of old stems, done in rotation across the plot, restores yields after one to two seasons.

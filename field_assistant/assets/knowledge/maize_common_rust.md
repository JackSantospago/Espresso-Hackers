Maize common rust: what it is, how to recognise it, how it spreads and what the farmer can do.

Maize common rust is caused by the fungus Puccinia sorghi. It is favoured by humid conditions with cool nights and moderate days. (Source: Wikipedia; Crop Protection Network)

Maize common rust symptoms: small light flecks on the leaf turn into raised, powdery, brick-red to golden-brown pustules about 1 to 3 mm long. Unlike many other leaf diseases, the pustules appear on both the upper and the lower side of the leaf. They can also appear on husks and stalks. As the pustules age, they turn dark brown to black. (Source: Wikipedia; Crop Protection Network)

Maize common rust spread: the rust spores are carried long distances by wind. The disease is favoured by high humidity, dew, and moderate temperatures (cool nights around 18 to 21 °C). The fungus needs living plants; its alternate host is wood sorrel (Oxalis) weeds. (Source: Crop Protection Network; Wikipedia)

Maize common rust damage: it is usually less damaging than gray leaf spot or northern leaf blight, but heavy infection before or at flowering can reduce yield, especially on susceptible varieties. (Source: Crop Protection Network; Wikipedia)

Maize common rust management: grow resistant varieties, which is the main control method. Plant early in the season where possible. Remove Oxalis weeds and destroy crop debris. Fungicides are used on commercial farms when many pustules (about six or more per leaf) appear early; for small farms, ask your extension officer whether spraying pays. (Source: Wikipedia; Crop Protection Network)

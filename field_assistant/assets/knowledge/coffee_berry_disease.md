Coffee berry disease (CBD): what it is, how to recognise it and what the farmer can do.

Coffee berry disease is caused by the fungus Colletotrichum kahawae. It attacks arabica coffee berries and flowers. It was first found in western Kenya in 1922 and spread across Africa; it occurs only at altitudes above about 1,000 m. It can destroy up to 80% of the crop. (Source: Wikipedia)

Coffee berry disease symptoms: on green berries, dark brown, slightly sunken spots appear and grow until the whole berry turns black. Some spots are pale tan, corky scabs instead. Diseased berries drop early or dry and stay hanging on the branch as black mummified berries. (Source: Wikipedia)

Coffee berry disease conditions: the fungus needs rain to spread its spores and high humidity to infect, with temperatures around 20 to 22 °C being ideal. Long rains during flowering and early berry growth bring the highest risk. (Source: Wikipedia)

Coffee berry disease management: replant with resistant varieties; in Kenya, Ruiru 11 and Batian resist both coffee berry disease and leaf rust. Prune and destroy infected branches, remove mummified berries, and use shade trees to reduce rain hitting the berries. Copper fungicides are used, but they are costly (many sprays a year); agree a spray plan with your extension officer or cooperative. (Source: Wikipedia; KALRO)

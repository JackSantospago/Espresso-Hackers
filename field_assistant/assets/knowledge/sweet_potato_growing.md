Sweet potato growing guide: climate, planting, harvest and storage.

Sweet potato climate: sweet potato grows best at about 24 °C with plenty of sun and warm nights, and does not tolerate frost. About 750 to 1,000 mm of rain a year is ideal, with at least 500 mm in the growing season. It is sensitive to drought about 50 to 60 days after planting, when roots start to form, and it does not tolerate waterlogging. (Source: Wikipedia)

Sweet potato soil and planting: it prefers well-drained, light to medium soils with pH 4.5 to 7. It is planted from vine cuttings or from shoots (slips) grown from roots. On very acid soils with aluminium toxicity, liming is needed. Roots are ready 2 to 9 months after planting, depending on the variety. (Source: Wikipedia)

Orange-fleshed sweet potato: varieties with deep orange flesh are rich in beta-carotene, which the body turns into vitamin A, so they are promoted where children lack vitamin A. (Source: Wikipedia)

Sweet potato problems and storage: virus diseases (such as sweet potato chlorotic stunt virus) and Phytophthora root rots are common. Cured roots can be stored for many months in cool (13 to 15 °C), humid conditions. (Source: Wikipedia)

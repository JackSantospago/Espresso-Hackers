Coffee brown eye spot (Cercospora leaf spot): what it is, how to recognise it, how it spreads and what the farmer can do.

Coffee brown eye spot, also called Cercospora leaf spot or berry blotch, is caused by the fungus Cercospora coffeicola. It attacks coffee leaves and berries. It is often a sign that the coffee plants are stressed, for example by poor nutrition, too much sun or drought. (Source: Pacific Pests, Pathogens & Weeds)

Coffee brown eye spot symptoms on leaves: round brown spots, mostly 5 to 10 mm wide, with a light grey or whitish centre and a yellow ring around them, like an eye. Spots appear between the veins and at the leaf edges. In cool, wet places the spots can join into large dead blotches. (Source: Pacific Pests, Pathogens & Weeds)

Coffee brown eye spot symptoms on berries: small irregular brown spots, usually under 5 mm, mostly on the side of the berry facing the sun. They can appear while the berries are still green. Infected berries may ripen too early, shrivel, and give poorer bean quality and taste. (Source: Pacific Pests, Pathogens & Weeds)

Coffee brown eye spot spread: the spores are spread by wind and rain splash and need water to germinate. The fungus survives on fallen leaves and plant debris in the field. High humidity, rain, warm temperatures and drought stress after flowering all make the disease worse. Seedlings in nurseries can lose many leaves. (Source: Pacific Pests, Pathogens & Weeds)

Coffee brown eye spot management: keep the plants well fed, especially with nitrogen and potassium, because hungry plants get the disease more. Give moderate shade (roughly 35 to 65%) instead of full sun. Prune for good airflow, make sure the field drains well, and remove fallen diseased leaves. In nurseries, do not crowd seedlings. (Source: Pacific Pests, Pathogens & Weeds)

Coffee brown eye spot and fungicides: copper fungicides or systemic triazole fungicides are used where the disease is severe. Copper can also kill helpful insects that control green scale. Ask your extension officer which product is registered and when to spray. (Source: Pacific Pests, Pathogens & Weeds)

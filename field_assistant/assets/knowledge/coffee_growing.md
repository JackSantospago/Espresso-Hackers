Coffee growing guide: climate, shade, planting, feeding, pruning and harvest.

Coffee climate: arabica coffee grows best at average temperatures of about 15 to 24 °C, with 1,200 to 1,900 mm of rain spread through the year and a dry period of no more than about three months. It is mostly grown at 1,200 to 1,500 m in the tropics (from sea level up to 2,800 m in some places). It cannot survive frost. Robusta coffee tolerates more heat and is grown at lower altitudes. (Source: Wikipedia; Naturland)

Coffee soil and varieties: coffee needs well-drained soil, and organic matter from mulch and compost keeps it productive. In Kenya, Batian and Ruiru 11 are recommended arabica varieties that resist coffee berry disease and leaf rust; SL28 and SL34 are traditional varieties known for excellent cup quality; K7 is a robusta for low altitudes. (Source: Wikipedia; KALRO)

Coffee shade: arabica prefers light or partial shade. Shade trees such as Inga or Erythrina, and bananas in the middle layer, protect coffee from heat and wind, add leaf litter and (for legume trees) nitrogen. Around half shade is a common target; very dense shade stresses the plants and favours diseases such as brown eye spot. (Source: Naturland; Wikipedia)

Coffee planting and first harvest: young coffee trees first flower 3 to 4 years after planting and take several more years to reach full production. In shaded organic systems, Naturland recommends no more than about 1,000 to 2,500 coffee plants per hectare, to leave room for shade trees. Food crops such as beans and maize can be grown between young coffee in the first years. (Source: Wikipedia; Naturland)

Coffee feeding and mulching: coffee berries remove a lot of nitrogen and potassium from the soil every harvest. Return coffee pulp and husks as compost, add manure, and spread mulch on the soil surface rather than digging it in, because coffee roots are shallow. Avoid deep hoeing near the trees; cut weeds low instead. (Source: Naturland)

Coffee pruning and rejuvenation: prune regularly after harvest, removing dead, diseased and crowded branches so light and air reach the bush, which also reduces leaf rust and other diseases. Old trees that yield poorly are rejuvenated by cutting them back hard to about 40 cm (stumping), roughly every 8 to 16 years. (Source: Naturland; Wikipedia)

Coffee harvest: pick only ripe red (or yellow, for some varieties) cherries, in several rounds. For washed arabica, start pulping the same day as picking. Dry beans on clean raised racks or tarpaulins, never on bare soil, to avoid mould. Collect fallen and leftover berries to control berry borer. (Source: Naturland; Wikipedia)

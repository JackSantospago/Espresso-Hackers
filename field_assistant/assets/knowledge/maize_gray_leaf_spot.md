Maize gray leaf spot: what it is, how to recognise it, how it spreads and what the farmer can do.

Maize gray leaf spot (GLS) is caused by Cercospora fungi. In Africa the main species is Cercospora zeina; elsewhere it is Cercospora zeae-maydis. Gray leaf spot is the most widely distributed maize leaf disease in Africa, and yield losses of 20 to 80% have been documented. (Source: PMC review of maize leaf pathogens in Africa)

Maize gray leaf spot symptoms: long, narrow, rectangular spots with straight sides, because the spots are held between the leaf veins. They are light tan at first and later turn grey, usually 0.5 to 7 cm long. Spots first appear on the lower leaves and move up. Many spots can join and kill whole leaves. (Source: PMC review of maize leaf pathogens in Africa; Crop Protection Network)

Maize gray leaf spot conditions: the disease needs warm temperatures (about 22 to 30 °C) and very high humidity, above 90% for 12 hours or more, with long periods of leaf wetness. The fungus survives on maize residue left in the field, so the disease is worst where maize follows maize. (Source: PMC review of maize leaf pathogens in Africa; Crop Protection Network)

Maize gray leaf spot management: plant resistant or tolerant varieties. Rotate maize with a different crop such as beans, so the fungus on old residue dies out. Bury or remove infected residue after harvest. Intercropping and adjusting the planting date can also lower disease pressure. Fungicides are expensive and rarely used by smallholders in Africa; ask your extension officer before spraying. (Source: PMC review of maize leaf pathogens in Africa; Crop Protection Network)

Maize gray leaf spot versus northern leaf blight: gray leaf spot makes narrow rectangular spots with straight edges along the veins. Northern leaf blight makes larger, longer cigar-shaped spots with pointed ends that are not limited by the veins. (Source: PMC review of maize leaf pathogens in Africa; Wikipedia)

Soil types: how to recognise your soil and how to manage it.

Soil texture: every soil is a mix of sand (coarse, gritty), silt (smooth, floury) and clay (very fine, sticky). Loam is a balanced mix and is usually the easiest soil to farm. Sandy soils drain quickly and lose water and nutrients by leaching; clay soils hold more water and nutrients but can become waterlogged and hard. (Source: Wikipedia)

Soil feel test: take a small handful of moist soil and rub it between your fingers. Gritty means sandy, smooth and silky means silty, sticky means clay. Then squeeze the soil between thumb and finger into a ribbon: the longer the ribbon before it breaks, the more clay the soil has. (Source: Wikipedia)

Improving sandy soil: add compost, manure and mulch to hold water and nutrients, and choose drought-tolerant crops such as millet, sorghum, cassava or sweet potato. (Source: Wikipedia)

Improving clay soil: add organic matter and use raised beds or ridges for crops that hate waterlogging, such as beans and sweet potato. (Source: Wikipedia; Infonet-Biovision)

Ferralsols and Acrisols: very old, highly weathered soils common in the humid tropics. They are often acid and poor in nutrients, with aluminium that can harm roots. They need organic matter, careful fertilising and often liming. (Source: Wikipedia)

Nitisols and Andosols: Nitisols are well-structured, iron-rich tropical soils; Andosols are volcanic soils with good structure and much organic matter. Both can be productive, but they hold on to (fix) phosphorus, so phosphorus fertiliser or manure is often needed. (Source: Wikipedia)

Vertisols (black cotton soils): heavy clay soils that swell when wet and crack deeply when dry. They are hard to work and drain poorly. Plant on raised beds or ridges and choose crops that tolerate some waterlogging. (Source: Wikipedia)

Leptosols and Calcisols: Leptosols are very shallow or stony soils that are hard to farm; mulch, water harvesting and hardy crops help. Calcisols contain lime (carbonates) and can be farmed, but crops may lack micronutrients such as iron and zinc. (Source: Wikipedia)

Luvisols and Chernozems: Luvisols are fairly fertile soils with good nutrient supply. Chernozems are very dark, fertile grassland soils with excellent structure and are among the most productive farmland soils in the world. (Source: Wikipedia)

Integrated pest management (IPM) and push-pull: controlling pests and diseases safely.

Integrated pest management (IPM) means using several methods together and spraying chemicals only as a last resort. The steps are: check the crop regularly, identify the pest or disease correctly, learn its life cycle, decide if the damage is serious enough to act, choose the safest effective method, and check afterwards if it worked. (Source: Wikipedia)

IPM prevention: choose varieties that suit your area and resist local diseases, rotate and intercrop, use clean seed and planting material, remove and destroy diseased plants and residues, and keep crops healthy with good soil fertility. (Source: Wikipedia)

IPM physical and biological control: hand-picking pests, crushing egg masses, traps and barriers come first. Protect natural enemies such as ladybirds, parasitic wasps, spiders, ants and birds, because broad-spectrum insecticides kill them and can make pest problems worse. (Source: Wikipedia; Pacific Pests, Pathogens & Weeds)

IPM and pesticides: if a pesticide is truly needed, use a registered product for that crop and pest, follow the label dose, wear protective clothing, keep children away, and store chemicals locked and away from food. Switch between different types of product, because pests become resistant when the same one is used again and again. Your extension officer can advise which products are approved. (Source: Wikipedia)

Push-pull farming: developed by icipe in Kenya for maize and sorghum. Plant Desmodium between the crop rows: its smell pushes stem borer moths away, it adds nitrogen, and it suppresses the parasitic weed Striga. Plant Napier grass or Brachiaria around the field: it pulls the moths away from the crop, and most larvae on Napier grass die. Both plants also provide animal fodder. (Source: Wikipedia)

Push-pull results: tens of thousands of smallholder farmers in Kenya, Tanzania and Uganda use push-pull, with maize yield increases of around 60% reported in studies. It also helps reduce fall armyworm damage. (Source: Wikipedia; Pacific Pests, Pathogens & Weeds)

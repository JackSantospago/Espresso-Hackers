Coffee Phoma leaf spot (Phoma blight): what it is, how to recognise it, how it spreads and what the farmer can do.

Coffee Phoma leaf spot is caused by Phoma fungi, mainly Phoma costarricensis and Phoma tarda. It is a problem in cool, high-altitude and windy coffee areas, for example in Brazil, where losses of 15 to 43% have been measured. (Source: Agrolink; Revista Ceres)

Coffee Phoma leaf spot symptoms: it starts on young leaves and shoot tips, usually in the upper part of the bush. Small dark brown spots appear and grow into larger dead patches, and the leaves become twisted or deformed. Later the tips of shoots and branches dry out and die back. Flowers and young fruits can also be attacked. (Source: Agrolink)

Coffee Phoma leaf spot conditions: the disease is worst when rain, fog, mild or cool temperatures and stressed plants come together. High altitude, cold winds and long leaf wetness increase the risk. Plants damaged during harvest or by wind are more easily infected. (Source: Agrolink; Revista Ceres)

Coffee Phoma leaf spot damage: the fungus causes leaf fall, loss of productive branches and fewer flowers, so the next harvest is smaller and yields swing more from year to year. Corrective pruning and extra treatments also raise costs. (Source: Agrolink)

Coffee Phoma leaf spot management: keep plants well nourished and balanced; prune to open the canopy so leaves dry faster; remove and destroy affected branches; choose varieties adapted to your area. In windy places, windbreaks reduce the cold wind and leaf damage that help the fungus. Check vulnerable plots every one to two weeks so you catch it early. (Source: Agrolink)

Coffee Phoma leaf spot and fungicides: where the disease is established, registered fungicides are used according to the label. Ask your extension officer which product is allowed in your country and when to apply it. (Source: Agrolink)

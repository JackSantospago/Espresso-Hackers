Bean rust: what it is, how to recognise it, how it spreads and what the farmer can do.

Bean rust is caused by the fungus Uromyces appendiculatus. It attacks common beans. There are hundreds of different races of the fungus, so a variety that is resistant in one place may not be resistant in another. (Source: Nebraska Extension)

Bean rust symptoms: small white or yellow spots appear first, then raised reddish-brown (rust-coloured) powdery pustules about 3 mm wide, often with a yellow ring. They are most common on the underside of the leaves. Late in the season the pustules can turn black. Badly infected leaves turn yellow and fall early. (Source: Nebraska Extension; Infonet-Biovision)

Bean rust spread: the powdery spores are blown by wind within and between fields. Under good conditions a new generation of spores is produced every 10 to 14 days. Infection needs long periods of water on the leaf, and moderate temperatures (below about 30 °C) favour the disease. The fungus survives on crop residue. (Source: Nebraska Extension)

Bean rust damage: the earlier the crop is infected, the bigger the yield loss. Late-maturing crops are at higher risk. (Source: Nebraska Extension)

Bean rust management: plant resistant or tolerant varieties suited to your area. After harvest, destroy or plough in bean residue and remove volunteer bean plants. Do not grow beans on the same field season after season; rotate with a non-legume crop for at least two years. Intercropping beans with cereals can reduce rust. (Source: Nebraska Extension; Infonet-Biovision)

Bean rust and fungicides: fungicide sprays work best when started as soon as the first pustules are seen. Ask your extension officer which product is approved and whether spraying pays for your crop. (Source: Nebraska Extension)

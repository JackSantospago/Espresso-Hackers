Soil pH and liming: acid and alkaline soils.

Soil pH: soil pH measures how acid or alkaline the soil is, on a scale where 7 is neutral, below 7 is acid and above 7 is alkaline. Most crops grow best between about pH 5.5 and 7.5. You can test pH with a simple test kit, indicator strips or a pH meter, often available through extension services. (Source: Wikipedia)

Acid soil problems: below about pH 5, aluminium dissolves in the soil and damages roots, so plants grow poorly even when fertilised. Manganese can also become toxic, and calcium and magnesium become scarce. Sweet potato is especially sensitive to aluminium. (Source: Wikipedia)

Why soils become acid: rain slowly washes out basic nutrients, especially in high-rainfall tropics, and repeated use of ammonium fertilisers such as urea and ammonium sulfate acidifies the soil. (Source: Wikipedia)

Liming acid soils: spread ground agricultural lime and mix it into the soil. Wood ash also raises pH and adds potassium. How much lime is needed depends on the soil, so ask for a soil test or advice from your extension officer. (Source: Wikipedia)

Alkaline soils: soils rich in lime (carbonates) can make micronutrients harder for crops to take up. Elemental sulfur, organic matter and acidifying fertilisers lower pH slowly. Sorghum tolerates pH up to about 8.5. (Source: Wikipedia)

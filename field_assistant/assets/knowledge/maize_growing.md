Maize growing guide: climate, planting, spacing, fertiliser, weeding and harvest.

Maize climate: maize grows best at about 15 to 30 °C and does not tolerate frost. It needs good rainfall or irrigation, but can be grown with as little as about 400 mm in a season with suitable varieties. Its shallow roots make it sensitive to drought, especially at tasselling and silking, when pollination happens. (Source: PlantVillage; Wikipedia)

Maize soil: maize prefers well-drained soils with pH 5.5 to 7 and plenty of nutrients; it does poorly on hungry, worn-out soils and is sensitive to salt. Mixing in manure or compost before planting improves yields. (Source: PlantVillage; Wikipedia)

Maize planting time: in highland areas, plant within about two weeks after the rains start; in lowland areas, dry-planting just before the rains is common. Planting early, at the same time as neighbouring farms, also reduces fall armyworm damage. (Source: PlantVillage; Pacific Pests, Pathogens & Weeds)

Maize spacing: a common spacing is 75 cm between rows and 25 cm between plants with one seed per hole, or 75 cm by 50 cm with two seeds per hole. On poor soils wider spacing (90 cm rows) is used. Plant seeds about 4 to 5 cm deep in moist soil. (Source: NARO Uganda (DINU); PlantVillage)

Maize fertiliser: apply a phosphorus fertiliser such as DAP or manure at planting for root growth. Top-dress with nitrogen (urea or CAN) about 3 to 4 weeks after germination, or when plants are knee-high; in high-rainfall areas split the top dressing in two. Exact rates depend on your soil; ask your extension officer. (Source: NARO Uganda (DINU); PlantVillage)

Maize weeding: do the first weeding about three weeks after planting, and keep the field clean until the crop covers the ground. Weeds compete strongly with young maize for water and nutrients. (Source: PlantVillage)

Maize harvest and drying: harvest maize on time when the cobs are mature and drying, dry the cobs or grain quickly off the ground, and store grain only when dry (about 13% moisture) to prevent mould and aflatoxin. (Source: PlantVillage; Wikipedia; Purdue PICS study)

Maize rotation and intercropping: growing maize after maize builds up leaf diseases such as gray leaf spot and northern leaf blight in the crop residue. Rotating or intercropping maize with beans or other legumes adds nitrogen to the soil and reduces disease. (Source: Crop Protection Network; Wikipedia)

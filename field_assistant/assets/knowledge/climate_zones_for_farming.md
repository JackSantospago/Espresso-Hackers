Climate zones and farming: which crops suit which climate.

Climate zones overview: farmers and scientists often use the Köppen system, which groups climates by temperature and rainfall: tropical (A), dry (B), temperate (C), continental (D) and polar (E). Knowing your climate type, and especially the length of your rainy season, helps you choose crops and varieties. (Source: Wikipedia)

Tropical rainforest climate (Af): hot all year with at least 60 mm of rain in every month, near the equator. Crops that like constant warmth and moisture, such as cassava, rice and robusta coffee, suit these areas. (Source: Wikipedia)

Tropical monsoon and savanna climates (Am, Aw): hot all year but with a clear dry season, when the driest month has less than 60 mm of rain. Most farming follows the rainy season. Maize, sorghum, millet, beans, groundnuts, cassava and sweet potato are common; drought-tolerant crops and early planting with the first rains reduce risk. (Source: Wikipedia; crop sources below)

Hot semi-arid and desert climates (BSh, BWh): little and unreliable rain; deserts often get only 25 to 200 mm a year. Sorghum and pearl millet are safer than maize here. Water harvesting such as zai pits, mulching and conservation agriculture help crops use every drop of rain. Without irrigation, deserts are not suitable for field crops. (Source: Wikipedia)

Tropical highland climate (Cwb): high-altitude areas in the tropics and subtropics, with mild temperatures, rainy summers and drier winters. This is the climate of arabica coffee (about 15 to 24 °C), common beans (about 17 to 27 °C) and highland maize. Cool, humid conditions here favour maize northern leaf blight, common rust and coffee berry disease. (Source: Wikipedia; Infonet-Biovision; PMC review of maize leaf pathogens in Africa)

Mediterranean climate (Csa, Csb): wet winters and hot or warm, dry summers, found on the western sides of continents at about 30° to 45° latitude, for example around the Mediterranean Sea. Crops grow mainly on the winter rain; summer crops usually need irrigation. (Source: Wikipedia)

Humid subtropical, oceanic and continental climates (Cfa, Cfb, Dfa, Dfb): these have cold or cool winters, so the growing season is limited by frost. Maize, beans, sweet potato and rice can only be grown in the warm season, after the last frost. (Source: Wikipedia)

Climate and crop temperature limits: maize grows best at about 15 to 30 °C, sorghum at about 12 to 34 °C, cassava at about 20 to 29 °C, sweet potato near 24 °C, and rice needs more than about 12 °C. None of these tolerate frost. (Source: PlantVillage; Wikipedia)

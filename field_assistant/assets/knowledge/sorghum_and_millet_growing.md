Sorghum and pearl millet growing guide: crops for hot and dry areas.

Sorghum: sorghum is drought- and heat-tolerant and needs less nitrogen than maize, so it is often a safer choice than maize in dry, semi-arid areas. It grows at about 12 to 34 °C, takes about 115 to 140 days, and grows on soils from sandy to heavy clay with pH 5 to 8.5. (Source: Wikipedia)

Sorghum problems: the parasitic weed Striga, birds eating the grain, many insect pests, and fungal diseases such as anthracnose. Push-pull with Desmodium reduces Striga in sorghum and maize. (Source: Wikipedia)

Pearl millet: pearl millet is one of the toughest cereals. It grows in drought, high heat and poor, sandy or acid soils where maize and wheat fail, especially in the Sahel of West Africa and in India. It is rich in micronutrients; iron-biofortified varieties exist. (Source: Wikipedia)

Pearl millet problems: stem borers, head miners, grain midges and grasshoppers can cause heavy losses. Zai planting pits with manure strongly improve millet and sorghum yields on degraded dry land. (Source: Wikipedia)

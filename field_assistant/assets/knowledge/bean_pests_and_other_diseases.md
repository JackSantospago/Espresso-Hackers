Common bean pests and other bean diseases: bean fly, aphids, bruchids, anthracnose and root rot.

Bean fly (bean stem maggot): small black-blue flies about 2 mm long lay eggs on young bean plants, and the maggots damage the stem, which weakens or kills young plants. To reduce damage, plant early in the season, improve soil fertility with compost or manure, mulch with straw or grass, and avoid planting next to older legume crops. (Source: Infonet-Biovision)

Bean aphids: small black aphids suck sap from bean shoots and spread virus diseases. Protect their natural enemies such as ladybirds. If needed, spray only the infested spots with a neem product. (Source: Infonet-Biovision)

Bean bruchids (storage weevils): small beetles, 3 to 5 mm long, that make round holes in stored dry beans. Dry beans well in the sun before storing (to about 12% moisture), mix them with ash or diatomite, and keep them in sealed, airtight containers or bags. (Source: Infonet-Biovision)

Bean anthracnose: dark brown sunken spots on leaves, stems and pods; the fungus is carried in seed. Use certified clean seed and resistant varieties, rotate for 2 to 3 years away from beans, and avoid overhead irrigation that splashes the leaves. (Source: Infonet-Biovision)

Bean root rots: seedlings stay small, turn yellow and wilt because the roots rot. Plough deeply, plant on ridges for good drainage, and rotate for several years (6 to 8 years where root rot is severe) away from beans. Seed treatment with Trichoderma can help. (Source: Infonet-Biovision)

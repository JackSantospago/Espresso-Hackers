Bean angular leaf spot: what it is, how to recognise it, how it spreads and what the farmer can do.

Bean angular leaf spot is caused by the fungus Pseudocercospora griseola (older name Phaeoisariopsis griseola). It is one of the most important bean diseases in Africa and Latin America. During epidemics, losses of 50% of the crop have been reported. (Source: Pacific Pests, Pathogens & Weeds)

Bean angular leaf spot symptoms on leaves: small grey spots that turn brown, up to about 3 mm wide, often with a yellow edge. The spots have straight, angular sides because they are stopped by the leaf veins, which makes them look like small squares or triangles. On the first (primary) leaves the spots can be larger and round with rings. (Source: Pacific Pests, Pathogens & Weeds; Infonet-Biovision)

Bean angular leaf spot symptoms on pods and stems: dark, sunken, oval or round spots that can join together. Seeds inside spotted pods become discoloured and can carry the disease to the next season. Badly affected plants lose their leaves early. (Source: Pacific Pests, Pathogens & Weeds)

Bean angular leaf spot spread: the fungus is carried in seed, survives in old bean residue and volunteer plants for a year or more, and spreads by wind and rain splash. It is favoured by temperatures of about 16 to 28 °C and by alternating wet and dry periods: wet weather lets spores infect, dry wind spreads them. (Source: Pacific Pests, Pathogens & Weeds)

Bean angular leaf spot management: use clean, certified or disease-free seed and do not save seed from spotted pods. Plough under or destroy bean residue after harvest. Rotate: do not grow beans or other legumes on the same field for 2 to 3 years. Grow resistant varieties if available in your area. (Source: Infonet-Biovision; Pacific Pests, Pathogens & Weeds)

Bean angular leaf spot and fungicides: copper or mancozeb sprays are used against the disease on leaves and pods in some places. Ask your extension officer whether spraying is worth it and which product is approved. (Source: Pacific Pests, Pathogens & Weeds)

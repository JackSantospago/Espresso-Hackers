Cassava growing guide: climate, planting, harvest and safe processing.

Cassava climate: cassava grows best at average temperatures of about 20 to 29 °C and needs a long warm growing period (at least about 240 days). It is very drought-tolerant and grows on poor soils where other crops fail, which makes it an important food security crop in Africa. (Source: Wikipedia)

Cassava planting: cassava is planted from stem cuttings about 15 cm long, taken from healthy, disease-free plants, before the rainy season. Take cuttings only from healthy plants, because virus diseases are carried in infected stems. (Source: Wikipedia)

Cassava harvest: roots are usually harvested within a year of planting, but they can be left in the ground and harvested when needed, which makes cassava a useful food reserve. (Source: Wikipedia)

Cassava mosaic disease: leaves show a yellow-green mosaic, become distorted, and plants are stunted; leaf stalks may bend into an S-shape. It is spread by whiteflies and by infected cuttings and causes huge losses in East and Central Africa. Use cuttings from healthy plants, grow resistant varieties, and pull out and burn diseased plants every week. Cassava brown streak disease is another serious virus disease, and root rots can destroy up to 80% of a crop. (Source: Wikipedia)

Cassava safety: all cassava contains natural cyanide compounds; bitter varieties contain much more. Peel, soak, ferment, dry and cook cassava properly before eating. Soaking for 18 to 24 hours removes about half the cyanide, and traditional fermentation removes most of it. (Source: Wikipedia)
